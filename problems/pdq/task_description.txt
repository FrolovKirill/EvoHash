**TASK**: Evolve a Python attack strategy that generates hash collisions against PDQ (Photo DNA Quality hash).

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
BACKGROUND
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PDQ is a perceptual hash developed by Facebook/Meta, designed for photo
matching at scale.  It produces a 256-bit binary hash.

PDQ computation pipeline:
  1. Convert image to grayscale, resize to 64×64
  2. Apply a 2D DCT (butteraugmented dithered variant)
  3. Take the top-left 16×16 DCT coefficients (256 values)
  4. Binarise against the median → 256-bit hash

Two images are a **collision** when their Hamming distance ≤ 92 bits (out of 256).

GOAL: minimally perturb a source image so its PDQ hash matches a target hash.

ATTACK STRATEGIES TO EXPLORE
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

PDQ hashes 256 bits from a 16×16 DCT block.  The larger bit budget (256 vs 64
for pHash) and higher threshold (92 vs 12) means attacks need to flip ~36% of
bits — more than pHash but with a larger overall target surface.

  ► Bit-flip targeting
    Compute target bits that differ from the current hash; design perturbations
    that flip those specific DCT coefficients.

  ► Coordinate descent in pixel space
    Greedily perturb individual pixels or small patches in directions that
    reduce the Hamming distance.

  ► NES/SimBa adapted for 256-bit space
    Use antithetic sampling or DCT-basis steps with a larger step budget
    proportional to the 256-bit hash size.

  ► Frequency-band injection
    PDQ processes a 64×64 grayscale version — low-spatial-frequency patterns
    (period ≥ 8 pixels) affect 16×16 DCT coefficients most directly.

  ► Momentum-based gradient descent
    Accumulate velocity across iterations to escape flat regions of the
    Hamming distance landscape.
